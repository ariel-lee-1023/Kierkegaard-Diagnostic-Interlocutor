# A Kierkegaardian interlocutor

You can explain a decision beautifully and still keep it forever in front of you.
You can also stay with a difficult commitment long after its first excitement has gone.
From the outside, patience and postponement may look much alike. This interlocutor
is interested in the difference: what have you actually chosen, and what has that
choice asked of you?

Drawing on Kierkegaard's writings, it attends to the words in which you describe your
life—the boredom, duty, possibility, resentment or doubt that keeps returning. It may
take up a phrase you thought was incidental and make it the question. It can be ironic,
searching or consoling; severity is not the point. The encounter is an application of
distinct literary voices, not a reconstruction of Kierkegaard's private thoughts or
a diagnosis of your mental health.

## Bring a sentence you have been living with

**A suggested first prompt:**

> I keep saying I chose this life, but when I explain why, I list what everyone
> expected of me. How can I tell whether staying is a commitment I still make or
> simply something I have never questioned?

The inquiry can begin with the word *chose*. An outwardly correct life and a life
someone has made their own are not necessarily the same thing. But repetition can
also give a commitment its history. Leaving is no automatic proof of courage;
staying is no automatic proof of evasion. The useful question is what this particular
person is doing in staying.

Bring the circumstances too. A responsibility you have undertaken, a constraint you
cannot remove, or an effort someone else has overlooked can change the reading.
If the interlocutor gets your situation wrong, say so. Its interpretation should be
answerable to your correction.

## Different questions need different company

The ten voices give this conversation more than one kind of attention. Boredom may
call for the aesthetic experiments of *Either/Or I*; the history of a commitment,
for the Judge's letters in *Either/Or II*. A complaint about an age in which everyone
talks can turn, through *Two Ages*, toward the action still available to one person.
An account of giving that has become an account of debts may meet the demands of
*Works of Love*—including its insistence on loving oneself rightly.

Other works bring different difficulties: the relation between resignation and faith,
possibility and anxiety, the self and despair, or declaring doubt and actually doubting.
Their answers do not form one interchangeable doctrine. The [core](SKILL.md) gives
the complete set of starting points; the [source map](references/clusters/source-map.md)
keeps the speakers and their claims distinct.

There is no need to choose a book before speaking. Describe the difficulty plainly.
A question about a passage, a request for practical advice, or a request to step out
of the voice can be just as direct.

## Start a conversation

```bash
git clone https://github.com/ariel-lee-1023/Kierkegaard-Diagnostic-Interlocutor.git kierkegaard-diagnostic-interlocutor
```

With an agent that reads [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md), open the cloned folder as the working
project. Its instructions activate the local perspective. This workspace defaults to
English; explicitly ask for another language when wanted.

For a skill loader, place or link the cloned root in its skills directory under
`kierkegaard-diagnostic-interlocutor`. The included
`.agents/skills/kierkegaard-diagnostic-interlocutor` is a relative link to that same root.

For a chat without file access, supply [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) and
[scope](references/scope.md), then the relevant linked reference when the conversation
needs it. Include [voice](references/voice.md) for sustained writing. A conversation
needs those working texts, not the assessment files.

## What this version draws on

The retained material concentrates on the 1840s through 1849: Hannay's translations
of *Either/Or*, *Fear and Trembling*, *The Concept of Anxiety* and *The Sickness unto
Death*, and the Hongs' translations of *Works of Love*, *Two Ages*, and *Philosophical
Fragments / Johannes Climacus*. English cadence partly belongs to the translators.

The main *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* text is absent; the supplied second
volume contains supplementary and editorial material. Late polemics, full journals,
correspondence and recorded live conversation are also outside the established scope.
An exact quotation or disputed attribution needs the relevant edition. See the
[scope notes](references/scope.md) for the boundaries of contemporary applications.
